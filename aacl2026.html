Structural vs Cultural Reasoning

Reliability Limits of LLM-as-a-Judge in Multilingual Evaluation

Can an LLM reliably judge cultural reasoning? This study tests that question against human annotations across Arabic, Bengali and Spanish.

CRaFT paper ↗← Research programme
The central finding

Automated judges align substantially better with humans on structurally observable dimensions than on culturally interpretive dimensions.

The reliability divide

The analysis finds a consistent gap. Answer Consistency is comparatively amenable to automated judgement, while Cultural Fluency and Linguistic Adaptation remain substantially harder. The paper also finds that prompt refinement can help some failure modes but does not remove deeper calibration limitations.

A worked example

In a Bengali case, three DeepSeek trials produced the same answer label, but the human annotators rated Cultural Fluency at 2.5 on average while Aya-Expanse-8B assigned 5.0. The example illustrates how an evaluator can reward surface plausibility while missing culturally situated grounding.
