Structural vs Cultural Reasoning

Reliability Limits of LLM-as-a-Judge in Multilingual Evaluation

Shehenaz Hossain · Houda Bouamor · Haithem Afli

← Cultural Adaptation in NLP

Study

This study examines whether LLM-based judges reliably evaluate cultural reasoning, comparing automated judgements with human annotations across Arabic, Bengali and Spanish.

The analysis separates dimensions that are relatively observable from those that require culturally situated interpretation.

Evaluation setup

Languages

Arabic · Bengali · Spanish

Three multilingual settings.

Judge models

6

Five open-weight models plus GPT-4.1.

Dimensions

5

Two structural and three cultural dimensions.

Dimensions

Answer Consistency

Stability of answer labels across repeated trials.

Explanation Consistency

Stability of reasoning patterns and justification structure.

Cultural Fluency

Whether explanations are culturally grounded and contextually appropriate.

Linguistic Adaptation

Whether explanations adapt naturally to the target language while preserving cultural stance.

Deviation

Whether explanations preserve cultural intent rather than drifting towards generic reasoning.

Key observation

Automated judges align more closely with human assessment on structurally observable dimensions than on culturally interpretive dimensions. The distinction matters because a fluent or internally consistent explanation can still miss culturally situated grounding.

In one Bengali example, human annotators rated Cultural Fluency at 2.5 on average while Aya-Expanse-8B assigned 5.0, despite consistent answer labels across repeated trials.
